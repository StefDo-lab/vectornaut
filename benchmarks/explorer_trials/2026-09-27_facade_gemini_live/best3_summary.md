1. **Tillandsia-trichome sintered calcite-silica lamellar glaze**
- What: a ceramic facade glaze of self-shingled sub-micron calcite-silica platelets (lamella thickness ~400 nm, interlamellar air gaps ~300 nm) embedded in a vitrified inorganic matrix on ceramic tile.
- Bionic inspiration: peltate foliar trichomes of Tillandsia usneoides (Spanish moss), overlapping mineralised cellular scales shielding desert leaves.
- Why it could work: stacked sub-micron lamellae give strong broadband back-scattering / interference for sunlight (claimed solar reflectance 0.95), while calcite and silica vibrational bands give high emittance (claimed 0.96) in the 8–13 µm window; all-inorganic, so no UV degradation of a polymer binder.
- Claimed benefit: surface temperature 33.6 °C vs 48.2 °C for aged paint at 38 °C air and 900 W/m² (−14.6 K, ~38 %); claimed to reach below ambient under peak desert sun.
- Main risks: differential thermal expansion between calcite platelets and silica binder causing interlamellar microcracking over thousands of thermal cycles.

2. **Hermetically sealed closed-cell sintered alumina tile (Cyphochilus-type)**
- What: a 3 mm sintered alumina/silica facade tile with ~58 % closed, isolated sub-micron voids (mean pore diameter ~280 nm), sealed under a ~1.5 µm dense flame-glazed surface layer.
- Bionic inspiration: the internal filament network of Cyphochilus beetle white scales.
- Why it could work: multiple Mie scattering in the closed-pore network (claimed solar reflectance > 0.96) plus high mid-IR emittance; the dense glaze keeps soot and moisture out of the optical voids, so reflectance is retained for decades; inorganic, UV-immune.
- Claimed benefit: net inward heat flux through a 20 cm concrete wall drops from 32 W/m² (aged paint, SR 0.65) to 6.5 W/m² (~80 % reduction).
- Main risks: stress mismatch between the dense glazed surface and the porous core leading to delamination under large day-night temperature swings.

3. **Desert-snail (Sphincterochila) calcitic lamellar sintered cladding**
- What: a 12 mm dense inorganic sintered cladding plate with alternating sub-micron (~350 nm) aragonite/calcite lamellae and closed micro-voids, no organic binder.
- Bionic inspiration: shell micro-architecture of the Negev desert snail Sphincterochila boissieri, which reflects over 90 % of solar flux.
- Why it could work: sub-micron refractive-index mismatch back-scatters sunlight (claimed solar reflectance 0.94) while carbonate/alumina lattice vibrations give emittance ~0.96 in 8–13 µm; fully inorganic for 20+ years.
- Claimed benefit: noon surface temperature 34.8 °C vs 53.6 °C for aged paint (SR 0.72), i.e. ~66 % lower conductive heat flux at noon.
- Main risks: moisture infiltration into the lamellae during rare humid or rain events causing micro-spalling when the surface is rapidly re-heated by the sun.
