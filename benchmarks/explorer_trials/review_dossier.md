# Candidate dossier: biocide-free, drag-reducing ship-hull coatings

Request: "Develop a bio-inspired coating for ship hulls that lowers frictional drag in water, works without toxic antifouling agents, and lasts several years in seawater."

Objective as framed: time-averaged hull frictional resistance over a ~5-year docking interval, including slime, macrofouling and coating degradation. Conventional baseline: a biocide-free silicone/fluoropolymer foul-release (FR) coating in service (light-to-medium slime after 12–24 months).

The candidates below were generated by an AI idea-search system and screened with simple 1D/2D physics models (release stress via compliance, Couette-type drag models, leaching flux). Authors' own estimates are given; treat them as claims to be checked.

| ID | Concept | Mechanism in one paragraph | Claimed effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Anisotropic permeable skin (lateral-line / xylem inspired) | A skin with sub-surface channels under a perforated sheet that is much more permeable streamwise than spanwise (Kx/Kz ≈ 40). Based on DNS results for streamwise-preferential porous walls (virtual-origin mechanism similar to riblets). | 4–8 % turbulent friction reduction on a clean hull; clogging by fouling is the main risk. |
| B | Whale-skin cm bladder panels | Centimetre-scale pneumatic bladders under a ~2 mm elastomer skin, periodically inflated (like aircraft de-icing boots) so the surface strain peels off juvenile barnacles; shear under a barnacle base ≈ 60–75 kPa. | Removes juvenile macrofouling without biocides; drag benefit via cleaner hull. |
| C | Closed-cell low-viscosity lubricant grooves | Streamwise microgrooves (~10 µm scale) closed into cells and fed from the floor with a lubricant whose viscosity is below that of water, so the interface gives real slip (typical infused oils are too viscous for slip). Variant: bladder-refilled cells. | 3–7 % friction reduction; lubricant discharge and ridge wear are risks. |
| D | Kelp-like lamellar self-shedding coating | Laminated silicone with weak interlayers; when fouling load raises the energy release rate above a threshold (G/Gc ≈ 1.25 fouled vs 0.31 clean) the top lamella sheds with its fouling. | Periodic renewal of a clean surface; releases ~20 g/m² silicone flakes per shedding. |
| E | Electrolytic plastron regenerated by cathodic-protection current | Uses the existing impressed-current cathodic protection to electrolytically generate gas that refreshes an air plastron in a microtextured surface. | Plastron slip; but anode chlorine production (~160 kg/day at full duty) acts like a biocide. |
| F | Broadcast-scree silicone-capped armour | Hard aggregate particles broadcast into a soft silicone FR layer and capped by a thin silicone skin: damage tolerance of an armoured layer with the compliance of FR; applicable with dock processes. | ~50 % lower barnacle release stress vs uniform FR; better cut/abrasion resistance. |
| G | Pack-ice-style crust for groomable soft gels | A thin stiffer crust over a very soft gel, sized with Pasternak-foundation theory so the soft layer survives in-water grooming loads. | Enables grooming of ultra-soft FR layers; stress in the gel reduced ~76 %. |
| H | Tiled tough skin on an oil-free supersoft bed | 30–50 µm filled-silicone or silicone-polyurea tiles, 0.3–0.5 mm wide with 20 µm gaps, on a 300–400 µm bottle-brush / low-sol PDMS bed (E ≈ 0.1 MPa). Tiles preserve the bed's compliance (release stress ∝ √(2w/C)) while resisting cuts and arresting cracks. Fish-scale / chiton-armour analogue. | 50–70 % lower barnacle release stress; ~0.5–1.5 % time-averaged friction benefit. |
| I | Palm-stem-like fibres normal to the hull in a soft silicone | Short fibres oriented normal to the surface in a soft matrix: shear-compliant (good for release) but tougher against sharp contact and indentation. | ~50 % lower release stress; ~0.5–1 % objective benefit. |
| J | Cartilage-like phosphocholine hydration-lubricated topcoat | A nm hydration-lubrication layer (phosphocholine brushes) that makes gentle, frequent in-water brushing (grooming) low-friction and non-damaging, so slime can be removed routinely. | Coating alone ~0.5 %; as a grooming enabler potentially addresses slime (5–10 % penalty). |
| K | Induction-switched LCST brush | A thermoresponsive (LCST) polymer brush switched by a contactless induction heating robot passing over the hull, causing a hydration change that releases slime. | Slime release on demand; heater grid alternative impractical. |
