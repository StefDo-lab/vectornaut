# Passive bionic facade for hot climates: best 3 concepts (ranked)

Reference case: a west facade in a hot-arid summer (Riyadh/Dubai type). Peak irradiance 650 W/m², 4.0 kWh/m²·d on the facade, air temperature 36 °C mean and 44 °C peak, indoor 24 °C, h_o = 17 W/m²K, 40 % window-to-wall ratio, block wall U = 1.8. The key finding is that **glazing dominates**. Clear glass lets in about 390 W/m² at peak. A wall under aged white paint lets in about 57 W/m², and only about 20 % of that is caused by the sun. So a coating alone cannot "significantly" cut the load of a glazed facade. It pays off on opaque walls and especially on roofs.

---

## 1. "Camel-fur louver skin": a ventilated Janus louver screen

**What it is:** A screen of aluminium louvers (AA6063, 25–60 mm deep, 1.2 mm thick) hung 100–200 mm in front of the whole facade, windows included. It is fixed on stainless brackets and left open at each floor so rising warm air can flow through. The louvers are set per orientation, the way some leaves turn edge-on to the sun. On the south side the depth:pitch ratio is 1:1, which blocks sun above a 45° profile angle. On east and west the ratio is about 2.5:1 with a 20° downward tilt, which blocks sun above about 20°. The top faces carry a PVDF white finish (AAMA 2605, solar reflectance about 0.8). The undersides and the face toward the building are bare or Al-Zn low-e metal, with emissivity 0.1–0.2. The louvers are roll-formed and prefabricated as cassettes.

**Bionic basis:** Camel fur can reach about 70 °C on the outside while the skin stays near 40 °C, because an air layer separates them. Shorn camels need roughly 50 % more water to stay cool. Paraheliotropic leaves (Eucalyptus, compass plants) turn edge-on to the high sun.

**Physics:** The screen stops direct sun before it reaches the building. Each louver absorbs 130–260 W/m² and warms only about 5–9 K, because air carries the heat away on both sides. Its low-e underside passes only 4–8 W/m² of that heat to the wall; an emissive underside would pass 25–50 W/m². The low-e faces also block longwave radiation from hot ground (about 50 W/m² net). Soiling of the top faces hardly changes the result.

**Benefit:** The effective solar heat gain coefficient (SHGC) of the glazing falls from 0.6 to about 0.10. The solar-driven heat gain of the wall falls by about 70 % compared with aged white paint. Daily facade load drops 50 % with clear glass and about 34 % with solar-control glass. Aluminium louvers and PVDF finishes have 25–40 years of track record.

**Risks:** Cost (€150–350/m²), wind loads, birds and dust on the louvers, cleaning access, glare, less daylight and view. Very low west sun (below 15°) still gets through.

**First experiment:** Two 1 m² west-facing test boxes, one with an aged-white face and one behind a 3D-printed or sheet-metal louver mock-up, with thermocouples and heat-flux plates. Log for 2 weeks. Cost under €1,000.

## 2. "Silver-ant mineral skin": an inorganic super-white coating

**What it is:** A potassium-silicate (or sol-silicate) paint without TiO₂. It is filled above the critical pigment volume with BaSO₄ and Al₂O₃ in mixed sizes of 0.2–2 µm, which leaves light-scattering air voids. It is applied in 3 coats totalling 300–450 µm on mineral render, concrete or fibre cement, and on roofs.

**Bionic basis:** The Cyphochilus beetle's disordered chitin scales and the Saharan silver ant's hairs scatter sunlight very strongly while emitting heat well in the mid-infrared.

**Physics:**
- BaSO₄ does not absorb UV, which is 5–7 % of the solar spectrum and which TiO₂ does absorb.
- Initial solar reflectance is 0.92–0.95, with emissivity about 0.93.
- The inorganic binder neither chalks nor softens at 70 °C. Acrylic whites get tacky at that temperature and pick up dirt, which is why they age to about 0.65–0.70.
- The target is an aged reflectance of 0.85–0.88, i.e. an absorptance cut from 0.30 to 0.13.

**Benefit:** Peak wall gain drops from 57 to 45 W/m², but the daily wall load only falls about 11 %. On an uninsulated roof (U = 3) the daily gain falls about 29 %. Silicate paint lasts 20 years or more, while acrylic needs 2–3 repaints in that time. The coating does not go meaningfully below ambient temperature in daytime once aged.

**Risks:** Desert dust caps the real-world gain. Thick silicate films can crack and do not bond to organic substrates. Bright walls reflect glare onto streets and neighbours.

**First experiment:** Coupons in 2 PVC levels, a commercial silicate white and an acrylic cool white as control. Measure solar reflectance, run the ASTM D7897 soiling test, a 70 °C dirt-pickup test and 6 months of outdoor exposure. Cost under €1,500.

## 3. "Compound-eye micro-louver glazing"

**What it is:** A 0.1 mm stainless or aluminium foil with photo-etched micro-louvers at a pitch of about 0.2 mm, sealed in the cavity of a double-glazed unit (IGU). The top faces of the louvers are bright and angled to throw beam sunlight back up to the sky. The undersides are dark matte for view contrast. Low-e coating goes on surface #3, the inner pane's outer face. Prior art: MicroShade, Okasolar.

**Bionic basis:** In insect compound eyes, screening pigment around each ommatidium blocks light arriving at an angle and passes light arriving straight on.

**Physics:** The design uses angle selection, not only spectral selection. It gives SHGC about 0.10–0.15 for high sun and visible transmittance of about 0.45 at normal incidence, where solar-control glass gives about 0.27 and 0.6.

**Benefit:** Heat gain through each m² of glass falls from 1.08 to about 0.48 kWh/day, and peak gain from 176 to about 78 W/m². Because the louvers are sealed inside, they need no cleaning and have no wind exposure. They last as long as the IGU, typically 20–25 years.

**Risks:** Weak against low east and west sun. Absorbed heat in the cavity warms the glass. It is limited to new or replacement windows. There is a cost premium and some haze in the view.

**First experiment:** Laminate louver foil into small IGU samples. Measure angle-dependent transmittance with a spectrometer or pyranometer on a turntable outdoors, and log the glass temperature.

**Discarded ideas:**
- Termite-mound ventilation: the classic story is mostly a myth, and the Eastgate Centre uses fans.
- Thermochromic dyes: they fail under UV within a few years.
- Hygroscopic "sweating" coatings: algae growth and salt loss rule out 20 years.
- Ribbed cactus textures: trapped reflections offset the gain.
- "Insulating" paints: an R-value of about 0.02 is negligible.
